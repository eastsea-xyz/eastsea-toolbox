# Problems before contracts

2026-10-06. The sixteen families below are evidence of needs, not a list to port mechanically. “Before” describes the user's alternative workflow, reconstructed from the cited product descriptions unless the source supplies a history. It is a design interpretation, not a claim that we interviewed those users. Original-product facts and limitations are cited at their point of use. Metrics keep their observation dates; addresses, operations, eligibility, votes, dollars and people are never interchangeable.

**Priority:** P0 = enables other user tasks; P1 = bounded consumer task; P2 = economically/data-dependent experiment; P3 = broad market or protocol-dependent research. Priority is an implementation decision, not a curated app ranking. **Layer:** select the lowest layer that actually enforces the relevant right. Existing account/protocol services are reused. Every cost claim follows [PRIMITIVES.md](PRIMITIVES.md), and every advantage is a test hypothesis until [MEASUREMENTS.md](MEASUREMENTS.md) is executed.

## Token exchange: AMMs, aggregators and order books

1. **Problem / who / before.** A holder wants asset B for asset A without finding a bilateral counterparty, handing custody to an exchange, or manually searching pools. Liquidity providers need a market that works without maintaining every price quote. More pools then create a separate routing problem; a resting order expresses a desired price rather than “trade now”. These are related user tasks, not one AMM mechanism. [UniswapX problem statement](https://blog.uniswap.org/uniswapx-protocol).
2. **Evidence of need.** Uniswap Foundation reported **$3T cumulative swap volume** on **2025-05-14** (milestone reached the preceding week). This is repeated traded value, not users or evidence of EastSea liquidity. [Dated roadmap](https://www.uniswapfoundation.org/blog/a-roadmap-for-programmable-liquidity).
3. **What still hurts.** Fragmented liquidity, approvals/wrapping, slippage, failed attempts and MEV remain documented burdens. UniswapX already offers signed orders, filler-paid gas and no user charge for unsuccessful fills; CoW already has uniform directed clearing and peer matching. Their existing answers deserve a stronger control than a plain V2 router. [UniswapX](https://blog.uniswap.org/uniswapx-protocol), [CoW](https://docs.cow.fi/cow-protocol/concepts/benefits/mev-protection).
4. **EastSea changes.** A1 batches bounded approvals and the action under one P-256 signature. A4 gates off-chain intents. B5 makes per-pool code and permanent resting orders expensive; one-second finality does not remove pre-inclusion reordering. R3 draw encryption cannot be called per-block order privacy. [Primitives](PRIMITIVES.md).
5. **Best layer.** **Contract:** [small pool](swap-pool/DESIGN.md) for always-available liquidity and [intent settlement](intent-exchange/DESIGN.md) for quoted/limit execution. **Wallet:** compare quotes, bound routing, display exact constraints. An unbounded on-chain CLOB is **not the default needed here**; if maker inventory/partial-fill/queue-priority needs cannot be met by intents, reopen that decision rather than pretending they vanished.
6. **Priority.** P2. No pool can create trading demand or inventory. Both quoted RFQ and delegated intent paths are gated on deployed 1271; the AMM self-batch is the earlier on-chain baseline. A maker's inventory allowance does not authorize a particular price. Originals may win on capital efficiency, order functionality and mature liquidity networks.

## Lending and borrowing

1. **Problem / who / before.** A holder needs liquidity without selling collateral; a saver wants a productive use of idle funds. Individual negotiated loans or selling the asset address different compromises. Aave pools collateral-backed borrowing, while isolated markets are a different risk boundary. [Aave borrowing/health](https://aave.com/help/borrowing/liquidations), [Morpho isolated markets](https://docs.morpho.org/learn/concepts/blue/).
2. **Evidence of need.** Aave's **2026-01-29** review reports **$950B all-time loans created**, **$55B deposits at 2025 year-end**, and over 100,000 liquidation events in 2025. Self-reported cumulative borrowing includes repeated borrowing; it is not unique borrowers. [2025 review](https://aave.com/blog/aave-2025-recap).
3. **What still hurts.** Borrowers must track changing health, rates and collateral prices; liquidation can transfer collateral with a bonus. Liquidation execution is competitive. The same source reports successful handling at scale—an original strength, not a failure to erase. [Aave liquidation documentation](https://aave.com/help/borrowing/liquidations).
4. **EastSea changes.** A1 simplifies add-collateral/borrow/repay batches. F1 changes deployment and per-position costs; fast finality shortens confirmation, not volatility or the availability of lenders/liquidators. External-price trust and token issuer risks remain.
5. **Best layer.** **Contract:** [isolated lending](lending/DESIGN.md), immutable per-instance assets/feed/rate rules and explicit liquidity limits. **Wallet:** show liquidation price/risk and repay directly. No protocol-run credit venue or founder risk administrator.
6. **Priority.** P2, gated on exact-transfer assets, authenticated price feed, observed market depth and permissionless liquidation tests. Original risk tooling and liquidity may dominate the smaller native implementation.

## Stable value: collateral debt and stablecoins

1. **Problem / who / before.** People want to quote, save and pay in a stable familiar unit rather than a fluctuating coin. A collateral holder also wants that unit without selling. Fiat reserves and collateral debt are competing answers; they solve neither solvency nor purchasing power simply by implementing ERC-20. [Circle unit functions](https://www.circle.com/reports/state-of-the-usdc-economy/usdc-the-worlds-digital-dollar), [Liquity borrowing](https://docs.liquity.org/liquity-v1/faq/borrowing).
2. **Evidence of need.** Circle reported **over $45B USDC circulation entering 2025**, published **2025-01-15**. Liquity reported **over $4B loans issued by its first anniversary**, published **2022-05-05**, anniversary **2022-04-05**. These are supply/loan-value measures, not people. [Circle](https://www.circle.com/blog/shaping-the-future-of-money), [Liquity anniversary](https://www.liquity.org/blog/april-wrap-up-happy-birthday-liquity-first-cefi-clients-access-defi-credit).
3. **What still hurts.** Circle disclosed **$3.3B** reserve-bank exposure at SVB during the **March 2023** depeg; a blockchain did not remove the bank dependency. Liquity V1 documents minimum debt, liquidation and redemption exposure, including a repayment premium if LUSD trades above the peg. [Reserve disclosure, published 2023-03-13](https://www.circle.com/pressroom/3-3-billion-of-usdc-reserve-risk-removed-dollar-de-peg-closes), [Liquity limits](https://docs.liquity.org/liquity-v1/faq/borrowing).
4. **EastSea changes.** A1/F1 improve transaction preparation and price state use, not dollar backing. An external stable asset requires a genuine issuer/bridge/support arrangement that this work has not identified. A native volatile DBLN CDP needs an external DBLN price and real redemption/liquidation liquidity.
5. **Best layer.** **Wallet/asset support first**, if a useful stable asset actually exists independently. **Experimental contract:** [collateral debt unit](stable-value/DESIGN.md) with explicit economic gates; it is not marketed as a proved dollar peg. Price discovery, legal redemption claims and reserve custody cannot be hidden as contract details.
6. **Priority.** P2 for borrowing experiments, P3 for a new stable asset. The original's trusted dollar access and liquidity may solve the user's problem better than inventing another unit.

## Payments, streaming and vesting

1. **Problem / who / before.** Employers/contributors reconcile spreadsheets, wait for periodic payroll and coordinate multisig transfers; grant recipients need predictable earned or vested access. Sablier's payroll article explicitly describes those manual alternatives. [Payroll workflow](https://blog.sablier.com/how-to-automate-crypto-payroll-in-your-dao-or-startup-without-a-cfo).
2. **Evidence of need.** That **2025-08-15** article reports Flow had handled **over $2.3M cumulative stablecoin volume**. This is one product's team-reported early activity, not the entire payroll market.
3. **What still hurts.** Fixed Lockup streams cannot be extended/topped up; Flow already addresses open-ended streams, so that criticism does not apply to all Sablier products. Withdrawals remain transactions. Sablier distinguishes protocol economics from a UI withdrawal/claim fee introduced **2025-02-03**; no blanket “original charges a protocol fee” assertion. [Flow launch, 2024-12-12](https://blog.sablier.com/introducing-sablier-flow), [fee distinction](https://sablier.com/organizations).
4. **EastSea changes.** Existing payment sessions authorize bounded native/ERC-20 transfers. A1 combines claim/pay calls; L1 supplies portable payment evidence; F1 favors finite funded schedules and retiring completed records. Sponsorship is pending, not free withdrawal today.
5. **Best layer.** **Account/wallet:** send, memo, invoice reconciliation and repeat capped payments. **Contract:** [already-funded streams/vesting](streams/DESIGN.md), with lazy accrual rather than a transaction every second. No subscription needs arbitrary permissions merely to send a capped payment.
6. **Priority.** P1 for funded streams; P0 wallet payments. Original Flow may win where open-ended credit-like obligations or top-ups are essential; native scope should not conceal this lost flexibility.

## Multisig and treasury

1. **Problem / who / before.** A shared treasury should not let one key holder unilaterally move the organization's entire balance. Signers need understandable effects, not merely more approvals of an opaque transaction. Safe already supports batching and contract/passkey owners. [Safe architecture](https://docs.safe.global/advanced/smart-account-overview).
2. **Evidence of need.** Safe Foundation reported **over $100B assets secured** on **2024-03-07**. This is asset inventory at the report's valuation, not organizations or users. [Dated milestone](https://safefoundation.org/blog/safe-usd100-billion-in-assets-secured).
3. **What still hurts.** Bybit's **2025-02-26** statement describes compromised signing infrastructure and deceived signers in the **2025-02-21** incident; this is not a demonstrated break of the Safe threshold contract. Safe documents that a malicious/broken guard can block fund access. [Affected-party disclosure](https://www.bybit.com/en/press/post/bybit-confirms-security-integrity-amid-safe-wallet-incident-no-compromise-in-infrastructure-blt9986889e919da8d2), [guard warning](https://docs.safe.global/reference-smart-account/guards/setModuleGuard).
4. **EastSea changes.** Existing P-256 `EastSeaVault` provides quorum, delay and bounded small payments. Wallet local simulation/evidence can display recipients and configuration changes. Account guardian recovery is a separate policy; A2's added owners can each execute, so it is not an m-of-n treasury substitute.
5. **Best layer.** **Existing contract + wallet:** [treasury reuse/adaptation](treasury/DESIGN.md). Improve queue retirement, offline approval handling and effect display in a new immutable version only where needed. No new founder-controlled custodian.
6. **Priority.** P1 contract lifecycle; P0 approval UI. Safe's modules, ecosystem and configurable routines may be substantially more useful than the minimal native treasury.

## NFT minting and fair distribution

1. **Problem / who / before.** Creators need bounded affordable issuance; buyers want an actual opportunity to obtain scarce items instead of repeatedly racing mint transactions. Batch-mint efficiency and fair allocation are distinct needs. ERC721A explicitly targets gas-efficient multi-minting. [ERC721A](https://github.com/chiru-labs/ERC721A), [Candy Machine](https://www.metaplex.com/docs/smart-contracts/candy-machine).
2. **Evidence of need.** ERC721A's README describes **8,700 public-sale tokens minted within minutes on 2022-01-12**. Metaplex reports Candy Machine minted **78% of Solana NFTs by September 2022**. Tokens/share of mints are not unique buyers; the older Candy Machine is deprecated in favor of Core, so benchmark version matters. [ERC721A](https://github.com/chiru-labs/ERC721A), [Core overview](https://www.metaplex.com/docs/smart-contracts/core-candy-machine/overview).
3. **What still hurts.** Metaplex documents bot-tax false penalties when common wallets inject Lighthouse instructions and transactions that appear successful while minting no NFT. Solana's **2022-04-30** outage report also ties extraordinary traffic to NFT mint bots; efficiency did not guarantee access. [Bot Tax warning](https://www.metaplex.com/docs/smart-contracts/candy-machine/guards/bot-tax), [outage report](https://solana.com/news/04-30-22-solana-mainnet-beta-outage-report-mitigation).
4. **EastSea changes.** A4 receiver hooks are a blocker for safe mint receipt; paid ownership/holder state limits capacity. R1/R2 require close-before-signing, not “zero randomness means safe”. A name, Mac registration or P-256 address does not prevent one person obtaining multiple chances.
5. **Best layer.** **Contract:** [bounded distribution](fair-distribution/DESIGN.md); deterministic allocation first, randomized assignment after R3/cutoff gates. **Wallet:** one deposit/claim preparation and unmistakable success/refund state. Do not charge failed users as if they received an NFT.
6. **Priority.** P1 deterministic entitlement after hooks; P2 randomized allocation with enforceable cutoff. Originals may beat native ownership costs and provide richer creator tooling.

## Fair token launches

1. **Problem / who / before.** A new community has no trading inventory, price or recruited market maker. Bonding curves bootstrap liquidity/price discovery; “no insiders win the first trade” is a second fairness need, not automatically solved by a curve. Pump states the cold-start problem directly. [Bonding-curve explanation](https://pump.fun/docs/bonding-curve).
2. **Evidence of need.** No trusted platform-wide total was established. A bounded original empirical preprint observes **166,098 launches over 2026-06-11–25**; latest revision **2026-08-03**. Collector observations are not a platform-certified census. [Study and methodology](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02795).
3. **What still hurts.** The revised study identifies recurring early-buyer cohorts, showing coordinated access remains a question; it does not prove every cohort abusive. Another revised original study (**2026-09-10**) warns that collector timeouts do not establish platform-side graduation failure. No invented universal failure rate is used. [Cohort evidence](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02795), [measurement warning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02823).
4. **EastSea changes.** B5 prices deposits, participant records and fresh holder slots. Draw-encrypted bids could reduce early bid visibility only with pre-signing closure and complete verifiable clearing; neither the SDK nor an enforceable cutoff adapter is delivered. Fast blocks still permit bidding/fill censorship and Sybil bids.
5. **Best layer.** **Contract:** [finite sale and refunds](fair-launch/DESIGN.md), with a separate gated sealed uniform-price design. **Wallet:** show sale cap, allocation/refund and delay; no protocol-native venue or central launch curator. On-chain curves are an optional comparison, not the starting requirement.
6. **Priority.** P2 bounded sale research, P3 strong fairness. Original continuously available curves may win on liquidity bootstrapping and responsiveness; a fixed-price/capped sale sacrifices price discovery.

## Gas sponsorship and onboarding

1. **Problem / who / before.** A newcomer with an asset or intent cannot act until acquiring the chain's fee coin. ERC-4337 explicitly targets programmable authorization, recovery and sponsored execution without a consensus change; bundlers/paymasters supply an alternative to a plain EOA transaction. [Standard, created 2021-09-29](https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-4337).
2. **Evidence of need.** BundleBear's first-party **2024 Year in Review**, ten-chain coverage, reports **103M executed UserOps**, **87% paid via paymasters**, and only **4.3M of 21.8M active accounts** used more than once. Paymaster payment includes user-paid ERC-20 gas, so 87% is not the free-sponsorship rate. Report publication date is not displayed; checked **2026-10-06**. [Original dashboard/research](https://www.bundlebear.com/research/year-in-review/2024).
3. **What still hurts.** Additional bundler/mempool infrastructure and sponsor availability remain; user retention is distinct from funded first use. Current bundler docs discuss alternate mempool propagation/validation constraints. [Mempool overview](https://docs.erc4337.io/bundlers/userop-mempool-overview.html).
4. **EastSea changes.** A1/A2 already permit atomic actions and relay of an added owner's signature. Every new-genesis ordinary transaction pays persistence fees; a contract cannot alter the fee payer after admission. F2 sponsorship is absent, and first owner/delegation setup is not magically zero-balance capable.
5. **Best layer.** **Account/wallet + funded relay now:** fee-transparent relay choice for already-configured owners, without on-chain reimbursement. **Contract later:** [open app-funded sponsor escrow](sponsorship/DESIGN.md), gated on native fee reservation/settlement. **Protocol upgrade:** alternate fee-payer rules; initial account setup also needs an explicit funded/sponsored path. No founder-funded default pool, registrar whitelist or censorship monopoly.
6. **Priority.** P0 funding/relay UX; P0 dependency specification, with upgraded sponsorship gated. Funders can always decline future subsidy; users need self-payment/other-relayer fallback.

## Prediction markets

1. **Problem / who / before.** Participants want an information-priced stake in a future outcome and counterparties beyond a single house. Conditional Tokens separate payout positions from the externally reported result. [Gnosis developer guide, 1.0.3](https://conditional-tokens.readthedocs.io/en/latest/developer-guide.html).
2. **Evidence of need.** Polymarket's closed 2024 presidential-election event reports **$3,686,335,059 volume**, opened **2024-01-04**, market end **2024-11-05**; checked **2026-10-06**. This is cumulative traded dollars, not capital deposited, people or all prediction markets. [Closed event/rules](https://polymarket.com/event/presidential-election-winner-2024/will-robert-f-kennedy-jr-win-the-2024-us-presidential-election).
3. **What still hurts.** Those rules use named news-source agreement and an inauguration fallback; truth interpretation and timing remain outside a contract. Gnosis binds the condition to a reporting oracle. Time-release encryption and random numbers cannot supply the actual outcome. [Event resolution](https://polymarket.com/event/presidential-election-winner-2024/will-robert-f-kennedy-jr-win-the-2024-us-presidential-election), [oracle model](https://conditional-tokens.readthedocs.io/en/latest/developer-guide.html).
4. **EastSea changes.** A1/1271/hook readiness changes trading/receipt friction, and F1 strongly prices per-market/per-holder state. Certified receipts can establish internal event bytes for clients; no general EVM historical-receipt verifier is supplied.
5. **Best layer.** **Experimental contract:** [bounded binary market](prediction-markets/DESIGN.md), immutable user-selected resolver, explicit deadline/void payout. **Protocol/SDK support** is needed for a future autonomously verified internal-event resolver. External markets retain external truth trust; no founder arbiter.
6. **Priority.** P3. Scope and classification stay outside beta financial-app execution. Original depth and breadth may win; narrowing to machine-verifiable questions is a different product, not general parity.

## Staking and yield vaults

1. **Problem / who / before.** A saver wants available returns without operating staking hardware or manually maintaining strategies, while still transferring/redeeming a position. Liquid staking and tokenized strategy shares solve different sources of return. [Lido recap](https://blog.lido.fi/recap-lido-q3-2025-tokenholder-update/), [Yearn vault overview](https://docs.yearn.fi/getting-started/products/yvaults/overview).
2. **Evidence of need.** Lido's **August 2025** recap reports a **9.7M ETH staking peak in 2024**. This is staking inventory, not users or an ERC-4626 usage count. It establishes staking demand; automated vault adoption is a documented product proxy, not a numerical census. [Team recap](https://blog.lido.fi/recap-lido-q3-2025-tokenholder-update/).
3. **What still hurts.** Lido's **2024-04-19** help article describes ordinary **1–5-day** withdrawals, possible slowdown and Ethereum queue dependence. Its reserve-fund article (**2026-06-24**) says that fund is not insurance. Yearn documents source/strategy and management/performance fee choices. [Withdrawal wait](https://help.lido.fi/en/articles/7858315-how-long-does-an-ethereum-withdrawal-take), [reserve limits](https://help.lido.fi/en/articles/5230602-what-is-the-reserve-fund-used-for), [Yearn](https://docs.yearn.fi/getting-started/products/yvaults/overview).
4. **EastSea changes.** A1 simplifies deposit/redeem flows; paid shares/checkpoints matter. **Contracts cannot stake native DBLN through an identified protocol interface** (ES0 H12). Node issuance is not automatically an ERC-4626 yield source or a promise to coin holders.
5. **Best layer.** **Contract only if justified:** [fixed-source yield wrapper](yield-vault/DESIGN.md) over actual independently funded/available returns. **Wallet-only reuse** if the underlying source already solves the task. **Not needed now:** native liquid-staking token. Real staking support would be a protocol upgrade with deposit/reward/slash/exit semantics.
6. **Priority.** P2 wrapper after source exists; P3 native staking, no assumed date. Original strategy availability and deep liquid markets are substantial advantages; zero-risk/guaranteed-yield language is absent.

## Governance and voting

1. **Problem / who / before.** Dispersed owners want a decision that reflects authorized participation and can cause the promised action, rather than rely on an informal poll plus an operator honoring it. Snapshot documents participation-cost and execution-oracle choices; Governor uses historical voting weights. [Snapshot Governor support](https://blog.snapshot.box/snapshot-is-now-home-for-governor-daos), [OpenZeppelin governance](https://docs.openzeppelin.com/contracts/5.x/governance).
2. **Evidence of need.** Snapshot docs report **400+ DAO custom-domain deployments**, a bounded adoption proxy checked **2026-10-06**, publication date unspecified. It is not total DAOs, unique humans, or proof of meaningful vote turnout. [Custom-domain documentation](https://docs.snapshot.box/spaces/add-custom-domain).
3. **What still hurts.** SafeSnap depends on a Reality.eth result, monitoring, bonds/arbitration and **24-hour cooldown**. Snapshot also offers sponsored on-chain votes now, so “gasless voting” is not novel. Anti-whale docs acknowledge concentrated voting power; hiding early votes does not make identities unique. [SafeSnap](https://docs.snapshot.box/plugins/safesnap), [Governor support](https://blog.snapshot.box/snapshot-is-now-home-for-governor-daos), [concentration](https://docs.snapshot.box/space-handbook/anti-whale).
4. **EastSea changes.** A1/1271 simplify authorized votes; B5 prices per-voter receipts and token checkpoints. R3 encrypted ballots require extra readiness work. A submitted tally hash is not a verifiable outcome, and current balances are not a prior voting snapshot.
5. **Best layer.** **Contract:** [bounded on-chain voting](governance/DESIGN.md), immutable checkpoint source and deterministic execution policy. **Wallet:** summarize proposal effects/weight/expiry. An off-chain opinion poll needs no custody contract; cryptographic tally aggregation is future gated work, not a correctness shortcut.
6. **Priority.** P1 bounded non-financial decisions with supported checkpoint token; P2 arbitrary executable governance. No founder veto or admin parameter changes. Original delegation, coordination and broader tooling may win.

## Identity and names

1. **Problem / who / before.** People want a portable memorable destination instead of copying long addresses or relying on one platform's address book. A name is a lookup right; “this is one unique trustworthy human” is a different problem. [ENS FAQ](https://docs.ens.domains/faq/).
2. **Evidence of need.** ENS DAO's **2025-01-15** revenue report records **$4,488,128 recognized registration revenue in Q4 2024**. This accrual-based paid-adoption proxy is not new registrations, cash receipts or people. [Revenue report](https://discuss.ens.domains/t/ens-revenue-report-q4-2024/20100).
3. **What still hurts.** Renewal/expiry can change the owner; ENSv1's grace is 90 days and ENSv2's documented grace differs. Registration retains commit/wait/reveal, including a 60 s minimum wait. A familiar name alone is not permanent counterparty identity. [ENS FAQ](https://docs.ens.domains/faq/), [registrar/version comparison](https://docs.ens.domains/ensv2/eth-registrar/).
4. **EastSea changes.** N1 already supplies immutable names, burned fees, expiry/grace and forward-checked reverse resolution. A1 can prepare bounded registration/update batches, but the 60 s commit age remains. Paid text records argue against duplicating profile data in every app.
5. **Best layer.** **Existing protocol service + wallet**, no new naming contract: verified resolution, contact links, renewal reminders and cautious expiry display. Apps read the canonical service. Private address-book aliases remain local. Personhood/credentials need their own trust model; not supplied by names, DeviceCheck or sub-accounts.
6. **Priority.** P0 wallet task, with **zero new per-contact on-chain state** for local aliases. ENS's integrations and namespace portability may be better; native names should not claim uniqueness of humans or merchant endorsement.

## Airdrops and claims

1. **Problem / who / before.** An issuer wants a fixed eligible set to receive entitlements without maintaining a custodial batch-paying operator or sending every transfer itself. The original distributor moves proof and transaction submission to claimants/relayers. [Merkle distributor](https://github.com/Uniswap/merkle-distributor/blob/master/contracts/MerkleDistributor.sol).
2. **Evidence of need.** UNI launch **2020-09-16**, eligibility cutoff **2020-09-01**: **251,534 historical addresses** could claim a user allocation, including roughly 12,000 with only failed transactions. Eligibility is not completed claims or people. [Introducing UNI](https://blog.uniswap.org/uni).
3. **What still hurts.** The immutable root cannot repair an allocation mistake in place; clients need leaf/proof availability and somebody must pay gas. The original already has a 256-claim bitmap and third-party submission to the named recipient; those are not native inventions. [Original source](https://github.com/Uniswap/merkle-distributor/blob/master/contracts/MerkleDistributor.sol).
4. **EastSea changes.** A1 combines bounded claims; L1 gives portable confirmed-transfer evidence; F1 means a new token-holder slot often costs more than its amortized bitmap bit. Sponsorship and proof-discovery UX can help, with F2 limits. Logs cannot replace the consumed-claim bitmap.
5. **Best layer.** **Contract:** [immutable claim campaign](claims/DESIGN.md), retained replay bits until closure/deadline; **wallet/data tooling:** locally verify leaves, obtain redundant proofs, claim to the authorized account. No central curator selects eligible users after publication.
6. **Priority.** P1. Publish original ties when its lean layout already wins. Root availability, wrong-recipient allocations and abandoned funds remain explicit issuer/recipient choices.

## Oracles

1. **Problem / who / before.** Contracts need prices or outside facts that deterministic execution cannot observe, instead of trusting an application's backend message without an authenticated reporting policy. Push and pull models change update costs, not the fundamental outside-data trust. [Pyth architecture, 2022-12-13](https://www.pyth.network/blog/pyth-a-new-model-to-the-price-oracle).
2. **Evidence of need.** Chainlink's **October 2024** keynote reports **$16.6T+ cumulative transaction value enabled**, defining it as USD value of transactions using its oracle. Team-reported repeated transaction value is not revenue, TVL, prices or users. [Original keynote PDF](https://blog.chain.link/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/smartcon-2024-keynote.pdf).
3. **What still hurts.** Pyth documents stale prices, confidence/outage handling and paid caller updates. Its **2026-05-26** Core announcement describes a July 31 commercial/API change and chain-support limits; do not assume free public data or automatic EastSea verification support. [Best practices](https://docs.pyth.network/price-feeds/core/best-practices), [update burden](https://docs.pyth.network/price-feeds/core/why-update-prices), [commercial change](https://www.pyth.network/blog/the-pyth-core-upgrade).
4. **EastSea changes.** A1 batches update/use; F1 prices frequent stored reports and signer sets. L1 can remove a hosted explorer's authority for internal receipts **in clients**, but no general on-chain receipt verifier is supplied. R1 randomness never proves an external price.
5. **Best layer.** **Contract:** [immutable authenticated feed](oracle/DESIGN.md), selected signers and explicit age/confidence/failure rules. **Wallet/light client:** verify EastSea-internal evidence directly. **Protocol/SDK research:** generalized on-chain internal-evidence verification. Authentication and independently true observations stay distinct.
6. **Priority.** P0 client evidence; P2 external feeds as a blocking market dependency. Original data coverage, operational delivery and economic accountability can be much better than a new quorum feed.

## Escrow and marketplaces

1. **Problem / who / before.** Buyers/sellers need asset-for-payment settlement without one trusting the other to send first; workers need a payment commitment before off-chain delivery. Atomic digital transfers and disputed physical delivery require different enforcement. Seaport documents the NFT trading problem; Kleros documents chosen arbitration/timeout behavior. [Seaport launch](https://opensea.io/blog/articles/launching-seaport-saving-the-community-millions-in-fees), [escrow workflow](https://docs.kleros.io/products/escrow).
2. **Evidence of need.** OpenSea's first-party retrospective says it passed **$10B cumulative trading volume in 2021**, checked **2026-10-06**, publication date unspecified. This is marketplace demand, not Seaport-specific volume or people. [OpenSea timeline](https://opensea.io/about-opensea).
3. **What still hurts.** Seaport's **2022-06-14** migration announcement estimated 35% gas reduction and removed account-init fees, but retained per-collection approvals. Physical-delivery disagreements require an acceptance/deadline/arbitration choice; an NFT trade receipt cannot prove goods arrived. [Existing improvement/approval step](https://opensea.io/blog/articles/launching-seaport-saving-the-community-millions-in-fees), [arbitration dependency](https://docs.kleros.io/products/escrow).
4. **EastSea changes.** A1 batches authorization and settlement; A4 gates signed listings/safe receipt; L1 authenticates payment/transfer evidence. F1 rewards small active records and bounded completion, not indefinite per-order history. None resolves “did the work meet the description?”
5. **Best layer.** **Contract:** [bounded escrow](escrow/DESIGN.md), exact contractually observable conditions or explicitly selected immutable arbiter. **Wallet:** local signed offer/effect summary and retained evidence. Digital market offers use the same settlement constraints without pretending to replace physical arbitration.
6. **Priority.** P1 bilateral funded escrow; P2 open signed marketplace after 1271/hooks. Seaport wins breadth and trading integrations; native scope wins only if completion/funding/evidence UX actually improves.

## Account recovery

1. **Problem / who / before.** Losing a device/key should not permanently lose money access; the prior self-custody backup is a seed phrase stored by the user. A convenient login is not a recovery plan. [Argent problem statement](https://www.argent.xyz/blog/crypto-seed-phrases-argent), [EIP-7702 scope, created 2024-05-07](https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-7702).
2. **Evidence of need.** No trusted recovery-completion total was established. Argent's **2020-06-19** disclosure supplies a bounded deployed-use proxy: **61 legacy guardian-less wallets** exposed to an edge case, **zero assets lost**. The site also shows later republishing metadata; retain the event date. [Disclosure](https://www.argent.xyz/blog/argent-bug-bounty-claimed-by-openzeppelin).
3. **What still hurts.** Argent's original **2021-11-16** article describes then-**$100+** L1 recovery costs, 48 h delay and cloud/company-held encryption-material dependence. Those are historical costs, not today's fees. Recovery security/liveness requires guardian or service availability; Safe already supports passkey owners. [Recovery workflow/trust](https://www.argent.xyz/blog/off-chain-recovery), [Safe owner types](https://docs.safe.global/advanced/smart-account-overview).
4. **EastSea changes.** A2 already supports k-of-n guardian signatures, default 48 h delay, cancellation and new P-256 owners operating the same address. **A stolen original EOA key remains valid**, even after added-owner recovery. Synced WebAuthn recovery and app-bound sub-accounts are proposed account/wallet work, not existing features.
5. **Best layer.** **Account/wallet**, no duplicate recovery escrow: guardian setup/rehearsal, clear recovery notification and fee funding; lost-key recovery uses existing policy. Theft needs a new-address migration that the thief might race, or a future account model whose root authority is revocable. That stronger model is **protocol research**, not a local `removeOwner` fix. [Account boundary](PRIMITIVES.md#capability-and-dependency-register).
6. **Priority.** P0 product/verification. Measure guardian signature count, delay and sponsor failure honestly. Native recovery does not promise theft protection, default central guardians or an automatic iCloud rescue.

## What coders should build first

First make existing authority, payments and evidence usable: batches, recovery rehearsal, native-vault approvals, names and receipt verification. Then implement finite funded rights: claims, streams, escrow and bounded voting. External-data/asset-dependent finance and future-draw fairness follow only after their gates are independently satisfied. Detailed ordering and three non-overlapping implementation lanes are in [PLAN.md](PLAN.md).
